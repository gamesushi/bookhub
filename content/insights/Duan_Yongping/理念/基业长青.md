# Built to Last

> **Products can be imitated; culture cannot**

---

## 📌 Concept Analysis

**Built to Last = A company's ability to survive long and thrive — it depends not on being smart, but on making fewer mistakes.**

Have you seen those shops that have been around for decades? They may not be the trendiest, but they survived. The reason is rarely that they did something extraordinary, but rather that they avoided fatal mistakes. Duan Yongping says that Bubugao has survived to this day "not because we possess any exceptional qualities, but because we made fewer mistakes." The secret to being built to last lies in this very sentence.

---

## 💡 Core Understanding

**1. The core of Built to Last is [[企业文化]] (corporate culture), not products or technology.**

These are Duan's exact words from a Bubugao corporate culture training session. Technology becomes outdated, products get surpassed, but a company's deeply rooted values and behavioral patterns constitute the real [[护城河]] (moat).

**2. Making fewer matters more than doing more right things.**

This aligns perfectly with [[Stop Doing List]] logic — knowing what NOT to do is more important than knowing what TO do. The essence of [[本分]] (duty/integrity) is simply not doing wrong things.

**3. Being built to last requires "Dao" (the Way) before "Shu" (technique) — if direction is right, even clumsy methods are acceptable.**

This shares the same logic as [[做对的事]] (doing the right thing) — first determine whether direction is correct, then discuss how to execute.

**4. The book *Built To Last* profoundly influenced Duan Yongping, but he maintains his own independent judgment.**

Duan has recommended *Built To Last* many times, yet he also said: "*Built To Last* is a great book, but it caused me to miss the early opportunity to invest in Apple, because the book described Jobs as a 'time-teller,' and I later realized that Jobs was actually also an excellent 'clock-builder.'" This shows that he reads books for thinking, not for copying conclusions.

---

## 🛠 How to Practice

**To judge whether a company can be built to last, look at three things:**

1. **Does corporate culture genuinely exist?** Not slogans posted on walls, but behavioral patterns that employees truly identify with and consciously follow. Duan says that Bubugao's core values are: duty (本分), integrity, teamwork, quality, continuous learning, and consumer orientation. These aren't empty slogans — they're genuine decision-making criteria.
2. **Is management "clock-building"?** Refer to [[造钟人vs报时人]] (clock-builder vs time-teller) — leaders who can establish systems and culture can better sustain a company's long-term operation than leaders who rely solely on personal charisma.
3. **Does the company have a "Stop Doing List"?** Companies built to last often know firmly which things they will never do. Refer to [[Stop Doing List]].

## 📖 Case Study Breakdown

**Bubugao: Using Corporate Culture to Navigate Competition**

At a Bubugao corporate culture training session, Duan said: "The average lifespan of Chinese private enterprises is only about 3 years... Lacking long-term vision and sustainable development aspirations, without building solid internal foundations of corporate culture, problems are only a matter of time." Bubugao's survival in the fiercely competitive consumer electronics industry is precisely due to this cultural foundation.

*Source: Business Logic Chapter (Duan Yongping Corporate Culture Training Session)*

## ⚠️ Common Pitfalls

- ❌ **"Being built to last depends on constant innovation"** — Correction: Duan says Bubugao follows the principle of "dare to come after others" (敢为天下后) — don't be the first to try something; wait for market validation before entering, then do it to the best of your ability. "Many successful companies were followers; pioneers have it tough." *(Source: Business Logic Chapter)*

- ❌ **"Large scale means being built to last"** — Correction: "Some Chinese people are overly fond of grandeur and quick success, always wanting to join the Fortune 500. Lacking long-term vision and sustainable development aspirations, without building solid internal foundations of corporate culture, problems are only a matter of time." *(Source: Business Logic Chapter)*

# 基业长青

> **产品可以模仿，文化不可以模仿**

---

## 📌 概念解析

**基业长青 = 企业能活得长、活得健康，靠的不是聪明，而是少犯错误。**

你见过那些开了几十年的老店吗？它们不一定是最时髦的，但它们活下来了。原因往往不是它们做了什么特别厉害的事，而是它们没有犯那些致命的错误。段永平说，步步高能活到今天，"并不是因为我们有什么过人之处，而是我们少犯了许多错误"。基业长青的秘密，就藏在这句话里。

---

## 💡 核心理解

**1. 基业长青的核心是[[企业文化]],而不是产品或技术。**

这是段永平在步步高企业文化培训课上的原话。技术会过时，产品会被超越，但一家公司骨子里的价值观和行为方式，才是真正的[[护城河]]。

**2. 少犯错误，比多做对事更重要。**

这和[[Stop Doing List]]的逻辑完全一致——知道不做什么，比知道做什么更重要。[[本分]]的本质，就是不做错的事情。

**3. 基业长青需要"道"先于"术"——方向对了，方法笨一点也没关系。**

这和[[做对的事]]是同一个逻辑——先判断方向对不对，再谈怎么做。

**4. 《基业长青》这本书对段永平影响深远，但他也有自己的独立判断。**

段永平多次推荐《基业长青》，但他也说过："《基业长青》是本好书啊，不过这本书让我失去了早期投资苹果的机会，因为书里说乔布斯是个'报时人'，而我后来才悟出来乔布斯其实也是一个很好的'造钟人'。"这说明他读书是为了思考，而不是照搬结论。

---

## 🛠 如何实践

**判断一家公司能否基业长青，看三件事：**

1. **企业文化是否真实存在？** 不是墙上贴的标语，而是员工真正认同并自觉遵循的行为方式。段永平说，步步高的核心价值观是：本分、诚信、团队、品质、持续学习、消费者导向。这些不是口号，而是真实的决策标准。
2. **管理层是否是"造钟人"？** 参考[[造钟人vs报时人]]——能建立系统和文化的领导者，比只靠个人魅力驱动的领导者，更能支撑企业长期运转。
3. **公司是否有"不为清单"？** 基业长青的公司，往往知道哪些事情坚决不做。参考[[Stop Doing List]]。

## 📖 案例拆解

**步步高：用企业文化穿越竞争**

段永平在步步高企业文化培训课上说："中国的民营企业的平均寿命只有 3 岁左右……缺乏一个长远的目光和持续发展的愿景，没有把企业文化的'内功'练好，出问题是早晚的事。"步步高能在竞争激烈的消费电子行业活下来，靠的正是这套文化内功。

*来源：商业逻辑篇（段永平企业文化培训课讲解）*

---

## ⚠️ 常见误区

- ❌ **"基业长青靠的是不断创新"** — 正解：段永平说步步高奉行"敢为天下后"——不做第一个吃螃蟹的人，而是等市场验证后再进入，把事情做到最好。"好多成功的企业都是后面跟进的，开路先锋不好做。"*(来源：商业逻辑篇）*

- ❌ **"规模大就能基业长青"** — 正解："中国的有些人比较好大喜功，急功近利，动不动就要做世界 500 强。缺乏一个长远的目光和持续发展的愿景，没有把企业文化的'内功'练好，出问题是早晚的事。"*(来源：商业逻辑篇）*

## 💬 原文金句

> "一个企业要长期做下去，要保持竞争力，要基业长青，就一定要有自己的文化。产品可以模仿，而文化是不可以模仿的。"（来源：商业逻辑篇）

> "我们企业之所以活到现在，并且还活得还好，并不是因为我们有什么过人之处，而是我们少犯了许多错误，失误率少成功的机会就大，我做投资也是这样。"（来源：商业逻辑篇）

> "《基业长青》是本好书啊，不过这本书让我失去了早期投资苹果的机会，因为书里说乔布斯是个'报时人'，而我后来才悟出来乔布斯其实也是一个很好的'造钟人'。"（来源：商业逻辑篇，2012-01-25）

> "'道'是先于'术'的，因为只要方向是对的，方法笨一点，也只是做得慢一点，不会走错路。"（来源：商业逻辑篇）
