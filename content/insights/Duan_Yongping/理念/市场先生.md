# Mr. Market

> **The market is an emotional quoting machine — it's not your teacher, it's your waiter**

---

## 📌 Concept Analysis

**Mr. Market = An emotional neighbor who comes by daily to quote you a price — quoting high when happy, low when depressed; you can exploit him, but you don't need to heed him.**

You and a neighbor co-own a bun shop. Every morning this neighbor knocks on your door, quotes a price, and says either he'll buy your shares or you buy his. Sometimes he's in a good mood and quotes high; sometimes he's in a bad mood and quotes low. You can completely ignore him — as long as you know what this bun shop is really worth, his mood has nothing to do with you. This neighbor is Mr. Market.

---

## 💡 Core Understanding

**1. In the short term the market is a voting machine; in the long term it's a weighing machine — short-term prices are determined by emotion, long-term prices by [[Intrinsic Value]].**

Duan endorses Graham's judgment: short-term stock prices are determined by supply and demand of buying and selling; in the long run they're determined by intrinsic value. Short-term fluctuations relate to trading behavior; over time they're actually irrelevant.

**2. The market IS "efficient" but not "promptly efficient" — sooner or later it reflects value, but not at every moment.**

Duan has a unique take on market efficiency: the market's efficiency isn't prompt — it's sooner or later. Sooner or later the market will reflect [[Intrinsic Value]], so it IS efficient. This doesn't mean the market is always right; it means eventually it will be right.

**3. Volatility is a friend, not an enemy — the more emotional Mr. Market becomes, the more opportunities appear for good companies at low prices.**

True investors like volatility. The more depressed Mr. Market gets, the more opportunities investors have — the greater the market fluctuation amplitude, the higher probability that ultra-low prices appear on good companies. When prices drop, what should come to mind isn't "should I sell?" but "is there an opportunity to add to my position?"

**4. Bull/bear market terminology itself embodies speculative thinking — [[Value Investors]] don't need to judge bulls and bears.**

Bull/bear market talk means looking at others' (the market's) performance. Value investing only cares about cheap vs. expensive, not others' opinions. Right business, right people, [[Margin of Safety|right price]] have no necessary relationship with bull/bear markets, though "right price" does indeed appear with much higher probability during "bear markets" than during "bull markets." When right price appears, buyers don't need to know whether it's currently a "bull" or "bear."

---

## 🛠 How to Practice

Facing market volatility, ask only one question: **Has this company's [[Intrinsic Value]] changed?**

- If unchanged, stock price drop = Mr. Market is in a bad mood today — opportunity to add position, NOT a sell signal.
- If changed (company fundamentals deteriorated), only then reassess whether to keep holding.

When facing declines, if you want to buy more AND have capacity to buy more, you should ENJOY the decline; if you don't want to buy, try to ignore it as much as possible — this is Duan's standard posture when facing market volatility.

During major bull markets, if you can't find enough cheap good companies, holding cash is actually the correct choice — though emotionally very difficult, requiring rationality and courage.

---

## ⚠️ Common Pitfalls

- ❌ **"Market decline means company has gotten worse — should sell"** — Correct view: Stock price drop just means Mr. Market is in a bad mood today, unrelated to the company's [[Intrinsic Value]]. "Good companies have nothing to do with whether stock price fluctuates. If you want to buy more and have the capacity to buy more, then declines are enjoyable; otherwise ignore them as much as possible." (Source: Duan Yongping Investment Q&A [Investment Logic Chapter], 2015-12-10; 2013-02-12)

- ❌ **"It's a bull/bear market now, so I should buy/sell"** — Correct view: [[Value Investing]] only cares about cheap vs. expensive, not others' opinions. "Thinking in terms of bull/bear markets IS speculation, not a matter of degree. Value investing only cares about cheap vs. expensive, not others' opinions, so shouldn't factor it in." The sole buying criterion is: relative to [[Intrinsic Value]], is the price sufficiently cheap? (Source: Duan Yongping Investment Q&A [Investment Logic Chapter], 2015-06-28; 2010-05-23)

# 市场先生

> **市场是个情绪化的报价机，不是你的老师，是你的服务员**

---

## 📌 概念解析

**市场先生 = 一个每天来给你报价的情绪化邻居，他高兴时报高价，沮丧时报低价，你可以利用他，但不必理会他。**

你和邻居合伙开了一家包子铺。这个邻居每天早上都来敲你的门，报一个价格，说要么他买你的股份，要么你买他的股份。有时候他心情好，报价很高；有时候他心情差，报价很低。你完全可以不理他——只要你知道这家包子铺真正值多少钱，他的情绪就和你无关。这个邻居，就是市场先生。

---

## 💡 核心理解

**1. 市场短期是投票器，长期是称重机——短期价格由情绪决定，长期价格由[[内在价值]]决定。**

段永平认同格雷厄姆的这一判断：短期股价由买卖供求决定，长期股价由内在价值决定。短期波动和买卖行为有关，长期来看实际上无关。

**2. 市场"有效"，但不是"及时有效"——市场早晚会反映价值，但不会随时反映。**

段永平对市场有效性有独特理解：市场的有效不是及时的，而是 sooner or later——市场早晚会反映[[内在价值]]，所以是有效的。这不是说市场随时正确，而是说市场最终会正确。

**3. 波动是朋友，不是敌人——市场越情绪化，好公司出现低价的机会越多。**

真正的投资人喜欢波动。市场先生越沮丧，投资人拥有的机会也就越多——市场波动幅度越大，超低价格出现在好公司身上的概率越高。下跌时应该想的不是"要不要卖"，而是"有没有机会加仓"。

**4. 牛市熊市的说法本身就是投机思维——[[价值投资]]者不需要判断牛熊。**

牛市熊市的说法是眼睛看着别人（市场）的表现，价值投资只管便宜与否，不管别人的想法。right business、right people、[[安全边际|right price]] 和牛熊市没有必然关系，但"熊市"时 right price 出现的概率确实会比"牛市"高很多——当 right price 出现时买的人并不需要知道现在是"牛"还是"熊"。

---

## 🛠 如何实践

面对市场波动，只问一个问题：**公司的[[内在价值]]变了吗？**

- 如果没变，股价下跌 = 市场先生今天心情不好，是加仓机会，不是卖出信号。
- 如果变了（公司基本面恶化），才需要重新评估是否继续持有。

面对下跌，如果还想再多买且还有能力多买，下跌就应该爽；如果不想买，就应该尽量忽略——这是段永平面对市场波动的标准姿势。

大牛市时，如果找不到足够便宜的好公司，持有现金反而是正确选择——尽管这在情绪上很难做到，需要理性与勇气。

---

## ⚠️ 常见误区

- ❌ **"市场下跌了，说明公司变差了，应该卖出"** — 正解：股价下跌只是市场先生今天心情不好，和公司的[[内在价值]]没有关系。"好的公司和股价是否波动无关。如果还想再多买且还有能力多买的话，下跌就应该爽，不然就应该尽量忽略。"（来源：段永平投资问答录(投资逻辑篇)，2015-12-10；2013-02-12）

- ❌ **"现在是牛市/熊市，所以应该买入/卖出"** — 正解：[[价值投资]]只管便宜与否，不管别人的想法。"想着牛熊市本身就是投机，不是或多或少的问题。价值投资只管便宜与否，不管别人的想法，所以应该不考量。"买入的唯一标准是：相对于[[内在价值]]，价格是否足够便宜。（来源：段永平投资问答录(投资逻辑篇)，2015-06-28；2010-05-23）
